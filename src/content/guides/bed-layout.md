This blueprint is for a standard **4x8 foot plot oriented facing south**, which maximizes the lower autumn sun angle and lets taller crops shade the heat-sensitive ones below them rather than blocking light they need.

The bed runs in three tiers from north to south. Tall, hungry crops anchor the north edge. Low, fast crops line the south edge where they catch full sun. The middle is filtered light and trap plants.

## The three tiers

| Tier | West to east |
|---|---|
| **North anchor** (tall, heavy feeders) | Broccoli + Thyme · Cauliflower + Sage · Cabbage + Sage · Tuscan Kale or Collards + Thyme |
| **Mid canopy** (filtered light, traps) | Buttercrunch Lettuce · Leaf Lettuce (shaded) · Swiss Chard · French Tarragon, Dill, Nasturtiums |
| **South border** (low-profile, rapid) | Spinach (overwinters) · Arugula (succession) · Mustard Greens · Radishes + Winter Savory, Parsley |

## Why it is arranged this way

- **North anchor.** Brussels sprouts, cabbage, broccoli, cauliflower, kale, and collards are the tallest and the hungriest. Putting them on the north edge keeps them from shading everything else, and the herbs tucked beside them (thyme, sage) confuse the moths that lay brassica pests.
- **Mid canopy.** Lettuces and chard appreciate a little relief from afternoon sun. Sitting just south of the tall brassicas, they get a light canopy during the hottest part of the establishment window. The dill and nasturtiums here pull aphids and caterpillars off the crops you care about.
- **South border.** Spinach, arugula, mustard, and radishes are short and quick. On the south edge they get unobstructed low-angle sun, and they are easy to succession sow into gaps as you harvest.

## The one hard rule

> **Fennel isolation mandate.** Do not place fennel anywhere inside this 4x8 layout. Florence fennel is allelopathic to brassicas and will stunt them, and it will cross with your dill. Grow it in a container or a dedicated separate bed, never in the matrix.
