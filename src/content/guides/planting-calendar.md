Zone 8a runs a garden all twelve months. This calendar walks the full year: cool-season greens and roots in late winter, warm-season fruiting crops and the bean relay through summer, and the brassica finish that carries the bed into winter. The relay bed this site documents (spinach, then beans, then brassicas) is one path through this calendar; the tables below give you the whole field to plan from.

## Two frost dates anchor everything

Every date here hangs off two anchors:

- **Last spring frost.** Around **March 27** in Zone 8a on recent local averages. Spring and summer crops count forward and back from this date.
- **First fall frost.** Around **November 10** in Zone 8a. Fall and winter crops count back from this date.

Find your own two dates (your local extension office or a frost-date lookup will give them to you), then slide the whole calendar to match. Hold the relative spacing: the start, sow, and transplant dates are staged so the bed never sits empty and never asks one crop to wait on another. The 8a example dates below assume a March 27 last frost and a November 10 first frost.

## Spring: cool-season greens, roots, and peas

The growing season opens in **late March**, around the last frost. The cool-season crops go in first, most of them direct-sown the moment the soil is workable. The two hardiest, peas and spinach, will take a sowing two to three weeks earlier if your soil works and no hard freeze is in the forecast; everything else waits for late March.

Spring greens bolt once the heat sets in, so harvest steadily and do not expect lettuce and spinach to hold past June. The overwintered spinach from the previous fall (see the fall table) is usually the first thing off the bed, ahead of this late-March sowing.

## Summer: warm-season fruiting crops and the bean relay

Warm-season crops wait for the soil, not just the air. Hold transplants and heat-loving direct sowings until the soil holds above 60°F, which in Zone 8a lands a week or two after the last frost. The longer the crop loves heat (okra, southern peas), the longer you wait.

The relay bed uses the **bush bean** slot as its interim crop: beans are sown after the spring spinach comes out, fix nitrogen through the summer, and are chopped in place in early August to feed the brassicas that follow. See the [succession and soil method](../succession-sequence/) for that handoff. As a stand-alone crop, you can sow bush beans from April for an earlier harvest, and Zone 8a's long fall leaves room for a second sowing of beans or summer squash in July.

## How to read it

- **Start indoors** crops (brassicas, tomatoes, peppers, fennel) need a head start so they go out as stocky seedlings instead of seed. Start them under cover and keep them watered.
- **Direct-sow** crops (greens, roots, beans, squash) go straight into the bed when the table says, spring crops into cool soil and summer crops into warm.
- The **harvest windows** overlap on purpose. Planned this way, the bed produces something to cut almost every week of the year, and the brassicas and kale carry the hardest part of winter.
