USDA Zone 8a gives you a long, forgiving shoulder season. The last frost lands in late March, around March 27 on recent averages, and the first hard frost holds off until roughly **November 10**, which leaves enough runway to grow three crops in one bed across a single year. The trick is treating the bed as a relay, not a single planting.

## The relay

This site documents one full rotation in a standard 4x8 foot raised bed:

1. **Spring spinach.** Cold-hardy greens that go in early and come out by late spring.
2. **Summer bush beans.** A nitrogen-fixing cover and food crop that rebuilds the soil the spinach drew down.
3. **Fall and winter brassicas.** Broccoli, cabbage, cauliflower, kale, and collards that mature into the cold, when their flavor improves.

Each handoff matters. Spinach leaves the bed light on nitrogen. Beans put it back. The brassicas that follow are heavy feeders, so they inherit a bed that is ready for them instead of one that needs rescuing.
