Every pairing in the [bed layout](../bed-layout/) is there for a reason. Companion planting in this rotation does three jobs: it masks crops from pests, it pulls pests toward sacrificial plants, and it makes efficient use of vertical and horizontal space. The pairings change with the season, so the tables below run spring, summer, and fall in turn.

## Spring pairings

The spring bed is greens, roots, and peas. The companions here protect tender leaves and feed the soil for what follows.

| Crop | Companion | What the companion does |
|---|---|---|
| Lettuce, Spinach | Radishes | Quick radishes mark rows and lure flea beetles off the greens |
| Carrots | Spring Onions, Leeks | Allium scent confuses the carrot rust fly |
| Peas | Carrots, Radishes | Peas fix nitrogen the root crops use; low crops share the bed without crowding |
| Beets | Bush-forming Lettuce | Shallow lettuce roots and deeper beet roots use different soil layers |
| Brassica seedlings (early) | Calendula, Alyssum | Early flowers pull in the first hoverflies and parasitic wasps |

## Summer pairings

The summer bed is the bean relay plus warm-season fruiting crops. The classics here are about pollinators, ground cover, and pest masking in the heat.

| Crop | Companion | What the companion does |
|---|---|---|
| Bush Beans | Summer Savory, Marigolds | Savory improves bean flavor and deters bean beetles; marigolds suppress root nematodes |
| Tomatoes | Basil, Marigolds | Basil masks scent and supports pollinators; marigolds deter nematodes |
| Peppers, Eggplant | Basil, Nasturtiums | Nasturtiums trap aphids; basil shades the soil and confuses pests |
| Squash, Cucumbers | Nasturtiums, Dill | Nasturtiums draw squash bugs; dill brings in beneficials |
| Okra | Southern Peas | Heat-loving legume fixes nitrogen beside the heavy-feeding okra |

## Fall and winter pairings

This is the brassica window, when pest pressure on the bed is highest. These pairings are the supporting layer of the [pest management plan](../pest-management/).

| Crop | Companion | What the companion does |
|---|---|---|
| Broccoli, Kale, Collards | Thyme | Aromatic oils mask the scent cabbage moths track |
| Cauliflower, Cabbage | Sage | Deters cabbage moths and cabbage loopers |
| Lettuces, Chard | Dill | Attracts predatory and parasitic insects; pulls aphids |
| Mid canopy crops | Nasturtiums | Trap crop for aphids; draws them off the brassicas |
| Radishes | Winter Savory, Parsley | Savory deters pests; parsley supports beneficial insects |
| Mustard Greens (south border) | (used as trap) | Sacrificial draw for flea beetles |

## The principles behind them

- **Aromatic herbs confuse pests.** Cabbage moths and loopers find brassicas by scent. Thyme and sage planted right beside the anchors break up that scent trail.
- **Trap crops absorb pressure.** Nasturtiums and mustard are meant to take damage so the crop you are growing does not. Inspect them; they are your early warning system.
- **Beneficial-insect plants pay you back.** Dill and parsley left to flower bring in the predators and parasitic wasps that police aphids and caterpillars for free.

## The one combination to never plant

> **Keep fennel out of the bed.** Florence fennel is allelopathic to brassicas, releasing compounds that stunt their growth, and it will cross-pollinate with dill. It is the single plant that does not belong anywhere in the matrix. Grow it isolated, in a container or a separate bed.

Companion planting is a supporting layer, not a substitute for the [physical and biological pest controls](../pest-management/). Use all three together and the brassicas come through the fall clean.
