A solid first pull, direct-seeded in the ground after the last frost on March 27
and lifted now that the tops have started to soften. Red, yellow, and white came
out together, with one lone shallot for good measure. They go onto cardboard to
cure with tops and roots still on, which lets the necks dry down and the skins
set for storage.

This first batch is for cooking and pickling. A second batch from a different
bed stays in the ground a little longer and gets pressure canned in late July.
